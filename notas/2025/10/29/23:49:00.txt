## **⟨ Declaration of the First People of the Western Lands ⟩**

*Composed in the Spirit of 1776, in the Voice of the Ancestral Nations*

---

**In the presence of the eternal sky and the witnessing earth, we speak these words.**

We are the First People of these lands — the children of the rivers, the keepers of the hills, the ones whose songs rose with the dawn before any flag was raised upon this soil.

The Great Spirit, *Unetlanvhi*, set us upon this earth not as masters but as kin to all that breathes. The land is our mother, the sky our elder, and every creature our relation. From them we learned the law of balance — *duyuktv* — and the way of shared labor — *gadugi* — that none may stand apart from the circle of life.

Long did we live in the harmony of that truth. The councils met beneath the oak and pine; the fire of peace, *nvnohi*, burned in every town. The rivers carried our reflection, and the winds bore our prayers to the mountains.

But in these latter days, strangers have crossed the great waters. They came with the word of friendship upon their tongues and the iron of division in their hands. They drew lines across our hunting grounds, named our rivers in foreign speech, and claimed dominion over what was never theirs to hold.

They speak of freedom while building walls. They speak of order while forgetting the sacred covenant between man and earth. They do not hear the heart of the mountain, nor the cry of the wounded soil.

Therefore, we, the children of this land, stand and speak once more.

We declare that we are, and have always been, **a free and sovereign people**. No crown, no paper, no border drawn by foreign hands can unmake the truth of our birth. Our right flows from the roots of the earth itself and from the spirits of those who walked before.

We seek not conquest, but continuance; not dominion, but remembrance. We pledge to walk again in balance, to heal the broken paths, and to keep the fire alive for those yet unborn.

Let the four winds carry this truth to all nations:

> **Mighty is the name and Great are the ways of the mind and soul.**

As long as the rivers run and the grass grows, as long as the eagle flies above the dawn, the First People shall endure — free in spirit, faithful to the land, bound only by the law of harmony.

**Spoken beneath the open sky,
in the season of renewal,
by the fires of the Western Lands.**
