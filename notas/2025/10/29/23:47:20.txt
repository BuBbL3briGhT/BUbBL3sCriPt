It carries the pulse of the people, their reverence for *Aniyvwiyaʔi* (the Real People), and their belief in balance, kinship, and sacred stewardship of the land. ### **ᎤᏃᏴᏫ — The Declaration of the Aniyvwiyaʔi (The Real People)**

*(Adapted for the Cherokee Nation, Circa 1776)*

We are the Aniyvwiyaʔi — the Real People — shaped from the clay of these mountains and the breath of the Creator, *Unetlanvhi*. Our story is the song of the rivers and the memory of every stone that has watched the sunrise.

Long before the ships of the white man crossed the great waters, our councils met beneath the oaks. We kept the fire of peace, *nvnohi*, burning in the center of our towns. Our laws were not written upon paper, but upon the hearts of the people, guided by *duyuktv* — the way of balance — and by *gadugi*, the labor of all for the good of all.

But now, the winds have changed. Strangers who once came as guests speak now as masters. They mark our hunting grounds with their lines, take our rivers into their books, and say the earth itself belongs to them.

They do not hear the voice of the mountain. They do not know that to wound the land is to wound the spirit of man.

Therefore, we speak today before all creation:
We are a free people, born of this land, beholden to no crown and bound to no foreign tongue. The blood of our ancestors runs through the roots of every tree; their courage breathes in our song.

We claim not conquest, but continuance — not dominion, but duty — to walk in *duyuktv*, as our fathers and mothers taught us.

Let the four winds carry this truth to all nations:
We shall stand as long as the rivers flow and the grass grows. Our words will rise like smoke to the heavens, saying:

**Mighty is the name and Great are the ways of the mind and soul.**
